No API calls and no large language model. The answers are written from my resume and played out step by step the way an LLM would produce them. One part is real: the small neural network in the picture, trained in your browser when the page loaded.

01TOKENIZE

LLMSplits text into sub-word tokens from a vocabulary it learned (BPE) and adds a start token.

HereSimulated. Words split on common endings like "-ing" or "-ation", and each piece gets an id from a hash. ⟨bos⟩ is the start token. The router network reads whole words instead.

02EMBED

LLMEach token id looks up a learned vector of thousands of numbers that encodes its meaning.

HereReal. The router network learned 64 numbers for each word (and its letter triples) when the page loaded. Each row is one of your words; the last row is the whole question after ReLU.

03RETRIEVE

LLMWith RAG, the question is embedded and compared with document chunks by cosine similarity.

HereKeywords and phrases pick the answer first ("agent island" beats "island"), then the closest example question. When neither finds anything solid, the router network decides, but only if it knows one of your words. The picture shows its guess either way.

04PREFILL

LLMThe prompt and retrieved chunks pass through every transformer block once, and attention decides which tokens look at which.

HereSimulated. The arcs are attention, with most of it landing on ⟨bos⟩: the "attention sink" real models show too.

05DECODE

LLMEach step scores every token in the vocabulary, softmax turns the scores into probabilities, and one token is sampled.

HereThe answer text is fixed, so it can't make things up. The top-5 list shows the chosen token next to plausible alternatives at temperature 0.7.

06BACKPROP · 👍 TRAIN ON THIS

LLMTraining measures how wrong the output was (the loss) and sends that error backwards through the network to nudge every weight.

HereReal, on the router network. 👍 runs one gradient step that makes this answer more likely for your question, and 👎 makes it less likely. The weights change only in your browser, and your feedback is replayed when you come back. The big version is the pipeline I run at Parker AI, where user reactions feed a fine-tuning queue for our self-hosted models and LLM judges.
